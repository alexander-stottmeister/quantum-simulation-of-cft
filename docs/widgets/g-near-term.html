G · The near-term point

The Letter’s claim of near-term applicability rests on one point: a D10 wavelet reaching accuracy \(\delta=\tfrac12\) on 128 sites, i.e. \(N=6\) and 256 qubits, certified at observation scale \(M=3\). Two conditions sit behind it. One of them is satisfied exactly at D10. The other is not satisfied at all, and the supplement says so.

The two conditions, separately

Regularity — met, and only just

The near-term figure is a wavelet-route, \(k=0\), one-particle statement. Along the wavelet route the quadratic rate \(\varepsilon_N^2\) at \(k=0\) requires the scaling function to be 3-regular, \(\sigma_K>3\), which first happens at \(K=9\) (\(\sigma_9=3.165\)). D10, with \(\sigma_{10}=3.406\), is comfortably over. D8, with \(\sigma_8=2.917\), is not — so the choice of D10 is doing real work, not decoration.

Localization — not met

A Daubechies scaling function of order \(K\) has support of width \(2K-1\); for the smeared observable to be localized inside one cell of the observation lattice one needs \(2^M\gtrsim2K-1\). At \(M=3\), \(K=10\) that is \(8\ge19\), which is false. The supplement draws the consequence rather than hiding it: the observables of that estimate “are wavelet details wrapped around the circle rather than strictly localized fields; the register size of the estimate is unaffected.”

So the near-term claim survives as a statement about register size, which is what the abstract actually says, and it does not survive as a statement about localized observables at scale 3. Fixing the localization means raising \(M\) — and the supplement notes that scale-5 observables would need \(N\approx10\) at the same accuracy, which is 2048 sites and 4096 qubits, no longer near term.
