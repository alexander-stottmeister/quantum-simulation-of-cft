D · The ground-state circuit

The lattice vacuum is Gaussian, so it comes out of a product state exactly, with no ancillas and no variational loop: \(|\Omega_N\rangle=U_{\mathrm{FT}}U_{\mathrm B} \bigotimes_k|{\leftarrow}\rangle_k\). It is the cheapest of the four steps, and this page counts it.

What this does not show. The exact counts on this page are not in the manuscript. A different network gives different constants and the same scaling. Gate synthesis into a native set — \(O(\log(1/\delta_{\mathrm{syn}}))\) per gate — is not counted at all.

Why it is this cheap

  • The state is Gaussian. \(H^{(N)}_0\) is quadratic, so its ground state is quasi-free and factorizes after a Fourier transform and a Bogoliubov rotation: \(H^{(N)}_0=U_{\mathrm{FT}}U_{\mathrm B}D^{(N)}_0U^\dagger_{\mathrm B}U^\dagger_{\mathrm{FT}}\).
  • \(U_{\mathrm B}\) is depth 1. After the transform the modes decouple into Nambu pairs \((k,-k)\), and the only correlations left in the vacuum are inside each pair. Disjoint pairs commute, so all the rotations go in parallel. The angles are a classical \(2\times2\) eigenproblem, \(O(n)\) arithmetic, computed once.
  • \(U_{\mathrm{FT}}\) is the fermionic FFT. Radix-2 butterflies built from matchgates, \(O(n\log n)\) gates in depth \(O(\log^2 n)\). Without translation invariance the fallback is a Givens network at \(O(n^2)\) gates and depth \(O(n)\) — a real difference, and the reason the homogeneous bulk is the case analysed.
  • It is not the dominant cost. Against the evolution’s \((nT)^{1+o(1)}\) and the measurement’s \(O(1/\delta)\) repetitions, \(O(n\log n)\) once is nothing. State preparation is the step that does not appear in \(\delta^{-3/2}\).
