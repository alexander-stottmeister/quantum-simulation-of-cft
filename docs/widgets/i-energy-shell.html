I · Energy-shell confinement

The static estimates hold on a fixed energy shell. The Heisenberg flow is generated by an unbounded operator, so the question is whether the state stays on such a shell for the whole horizon. Lemma S5 says it does — but the shell grows like \(e^{c_kt}\), and only for \(k=0\) is \(c_k\) zero.

The shell against the lattice cutoff

Statement: Lemma S5 and the quantitative Duhamel estimate; the horizon factor is what appears in Theorem S1(ii). The consequence is visible in widget H, panel (b).

What this does not show. \(c_k\) is a control here, not a computed number. The supplement gives only a one-particle bound, \(c_{\sigma,k}\le C_L\,\sigma|k|\langle k\rangle^{\sigma+2}\), and does not evaluate \(C_L\); the initial shell \(2^{M}\) is likewise a stand-in for the energy the smeared observables actually put into the state. What is not adjustable is \(c_0=0\).

Why the horizon enters this way

  1. Uniform energy bounds. \(\lVert L^{(N)}_k\Psi\rVert\le b_k\lVert\mathcal N^{(N)}\Psi\rVert\) with \(b_k\) independent of the scale \(N\). These are the lattice counterparts of the Virasoro linear energy bounds of Buchholz–Schulz-Mirbach and Carpi; that they hold uniformly in the scale is the point, and it is what closes the gap in the published proof of Theorem 6.3 of the companion article.
  2. Grönwall. The form bound on \(i[L^{(N)}_0,H^{(N)}_k]\) gives \(\lVert(\mathcal N^{(N)})^pe^{itH^{(N)}_k}\Psi\rVert\le e^{pc_k|t|}\lVert(\mathcal N^{(N)})^p\Psi\rVert\). At \(k=0\) the commutator vanishes, so \(c_0=0\) and the shell is dynamically invariant.
  3. Duhamel. \((U^{(N)}_t-U_t)\Psi=i\int_0^tU^{(N)}_{t-s}(H^{(N)}_k-H_k)U_s\Psi\,\mathrm ds\). Inserting the relative bound \(\varkappa_N\) and the energy growth, then integrating, gives \(\varkappa_N\Lambda_{k,p}(t)\lVert\mathcal N^p\Psi\rVert\). This integral is where the horizon factor comes from, and it is the only rate-carrier in the argument.
  4. Not Trotter–Kato. Strong-resolvent convergence plus the uniform energy bounds already give \(U^{(N)}_t\to U_t\) strongly and uniformly on compacts, but that argument carries no rate and is used in the supplement only as a consistency check.

Two properties of \(k=0,\pm1\) are easy to run together and are not the same. The off-diagonal Hilbert–Schmidt part vanishes for all three Möbius generators (Lemma S2). Dynamical shell-invariance and the linear-in-\(T\) prefactor hold for \(k=0\) only.
