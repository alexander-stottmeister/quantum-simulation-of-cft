The three layers

  1. The staggered lattice. \(\Lambda_N=\varepsilon_N\{-L_N,\dots,L_N-1\}\) with \(\varepsilon_NL_N=L\), carrying the two-component Dirac fermion \(\psi^{(1)},\psi^{(2)}\) and the hopping Hamiltonian with mass coupling \(\lambda_N\). Number of sites: \(n=2^{N+1}\).
  2. The halved lattice. Setting \(a_x\equiv\psi^{(1)}_x\) and \(a_{x+\varepsilon_{N+1}}\equiv\psi^{(2)\dagger}_x\) spreads the two components over \(\Lambda_{N+1}\), which has half the spacing and \(2n\) sites — one fermion mode each.
  3. The qubit chain. Jordan–Wigner, \(a_j\equiv\sigma^x_{-L}\cdots\sigma^x_{j-\varepsilon_{N+1}}\tfrac12(\sigma^z_j+i\sigma^y_j)\), makes the Hamiltonian an XY chain in a transverse field on \(2n\) qubits:
$$H^{(N)}_{0}=\tfrac{L_N}{2\pi}\sum_{j\in\Lambda_{N+1}} \bigl(\sigma^z_j\sigma^z_{j+\varepsilon_{N+1}}-\sigma^y_j\sigma^y_{j+\varepsilon_{N+1}} +\lambda_N\sigma^x_j\bigr)+B_N .$$

Periodic or antiperiodic boundary conditions pick the Ramond or the Neveu–Schwarz sector; the real chiral components of the massless theory give \(c=\tfrac12\), and the complex chiral component gives \(c=1\). The Koo–Saleur construction on top of this model realizes \(c=0,\tfrac12,1\) depending on the representation.

The Letter notes that there is “considerable room for optimization here” and that more sophisticated fermion encodings would improve the requirement. The \(2n\) drawn here is the cost of the encoding actually analysed.
