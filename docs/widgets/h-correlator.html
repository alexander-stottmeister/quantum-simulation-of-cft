H · Correlator convergence

Everything else on this site is a bound. This is the one place where the rate is measured: the two-point function of smeared fields evolved by \(H_k^{(N)}=L_k^{(N)}+L_{-k}^{(N)}\), against a continuum reference, computed in your browser. The per-step ratio goes to 4, which is \(\varepsilon_N^2=4^{-N}\); and at \(k=2\) the error turns upward late in time, which is the energy shell reaching the cutoff.

\(|C^{(N)}(t)-C(t)|\), against the scale and against time

Statement: this is the numerical check of Theorem S1(ii) at the correlator level, and the mechanism it makes visible is Lemma S5. It is Fig. S7 of the supplement, recomputed. The reference implementation is numerics/correlator_convergence.py.

What this does not show. It is one correlator, of one fixed pair of smeared fields, in the free chiral theory, against a truncated continuum reference at momentum cutoff \(2^{11}\) — not the bound of Theorem S1, and not a test of its prefactor. A measured rate agreeing with a proved rate is corroboration, not proof.

Does this page agree with the committed data?

The browser diagonalizes the same one-particle symbol that tools/make_data.py hands to numpy’s eigh, by a different route: the symbol connects \(l\leftrightarrow l\pm k\) only, so it splits into \(k\) independent symmetric tridiagonal chains, and an implicit-QL sweep carries along just the two projections the correlator needs. The two agree to about twelve figures on the correlator itself, and therefore to about six on the tiny difference the plot shows — which is why the tolerance below carries an absolute floor as well as a relative one.

What is being computed

Modes \(l\in\mathbb Z+\tfrac12\) with \(|l|<\Lambda\); at lattice scale \(N\), \(\Lambda_N=2^N\), so there are \(2^{N+1}=n\) modes, one per site. The one-particle symbol of the simulated generator \(H_k=L_k+L_{-k}\) is \(s=B+B^{\mathsf T}\) with

$$B[l+k,\,l]=\varepsilon_N^{-1}\sin\bigl(\varepsilon_N(l+\tfrac k2)\bigr)\ \ \text{(lattice)}, \qquad B[l+k,\,l]=l+\tfrac k2\ \ \text{(continuum)}.$$

The vacuum is the Dirac sea \(P_{<}\), the projection onto \(l<0\). The observables are a fixed pair of coefficient vectors supported on \(|l|<8\) — observation scale \(M=3\) — identical at every scale, because the momentum-cutoff scaling map acts as the identity on retained modes. For a number-conserving bilinear the Heisenberg evolute of a field is a field, so

$$C^{(N)}(t)=\bigl\langle\,e^{its}g\,,\ P_{<}f\,\bigr\rangle =\sum_j e^{-itw_j}\,a_j\,b_j,\qquad a=V^{\mathsf T}g,\quad b=V^{\mathsf T}P_{<}f.$$

Expanding \(\varepsilon^{-1}\sin(\varepsilon x)=x-\varepsilon^2x^3/6+\cdots\) shows the symbol error is \(O(4^{-N})\), and the measured correlator error follows it. That expansion is the whole of Lemma S1; widget C draws it.

The two features the supplement points at

  • The per-step ratio 4.00. A least-squares fit of \(\log_2\) error against \(N\) over \(N=6,\dots,10\) gives a ratio of 4.00 to three figures for each of \(k=0,1,2\). Fitting from \(N=4\) instead gives about 4.02 to 4.32, because the smallest lattices are not yet asymptotic — a reason to quote the range of the fit.
  • The \(k=2\) late-time growth. At \(N=8\) the error for \(k=0,1\) oscillates under a bounded envelope, while for \(k=2\) it rises by four orders of magnitude around \(t\approx1.5\ldots2\). That is the energy shell \(e^{c_kt}\) of Lemma S5 reaching the lattice cutoff: once the evolved state has weight at the edge of the retained band, the lattice symbol and the continuum symbol no longer agree on where the state lives. Widget I is about that mechanism.
