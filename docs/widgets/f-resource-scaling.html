F · Resource scaling

The headline number of the Letter is \(\delta^{-3/2}\), and it is a product of two things that come from opposite ends of the algorithm: a Heisenberg-limited measurement cost \(\delta^{-1}\) from phase estimation, and a discretization cost \(\delta^{-1/2}\) set by the \(1/n^2\) convergence of the lattice approximation. This page separates them.

What this does not show. It does not show a gate count. Where each exponent comes from

FactorExponentWhy
measurement, step 4\(\delta^{-1}\) Heisenberg-limited phase estimation: \(O(1/\delta)\) controlled-evolution calls to the per-run circuit, with \(O(\log\tfrac1\delta)\) readout ancillas.
discretization\(\delta^{-1/2}\) \(C\varepsilon_N^2=C4^{-N}\le\delta\) gives \(N\ge\tfrac12\log_2(C/\delta)\), hence \(n=2^{N+1}\lesssim2C^{1/2}\delta^{-1/2}\). The per-run cost \((nT)^{1+o(1)}\) is linear in \(n\).
product\(\delta^{-3/2}\) \(O(1/\delta)\times(T\delta^{-1/2})^{1+o(1)}=T^{1+o(1)}\delta^{-3/2-o(1)}\).
wavelet route, \(k\neq0\), not re-centred\(\delta^{-2}\) The one-particle rate is \(\varepsilon_N\) rather than \(\varepsilon_N^2\), so \(n\sim C_T\delta^{-1}\) and the product becomes \(\delta^{-2}\). Localization in real space is paid for in qubits unless the comparison is re-centred.

The calibration is one measured number, and it is not a correlator bound. \(\delta_0\approx0.4\) at \(N=6\), \(M=3\) is read off the one-particle, wavelet-route, \(k=0\) data of Fig. S2. The curve drawn here is the exponent statement of the momentum-cutoff route anchored at that point; the prefactor \(C_T=2C^{1/2}\) is not independently computed. A reader who wants a bound with every constant should use Theorem S1(i) directly — widget E evaluates it — and will find a different, smaller number, because it is a different quantity.
