E · The error budget

Theorem S1 has two halves and they are not the same kind of statement. For the chiral Hamiltonian evolution, \(k=0\), the bound is proved with every constant, and this page evaluates it. For the local conformal transformations, \(k\neq0\), the bound is assembled in the supplement and its prefactor is not computed — so that branch is drawn as a shape with \(C_0\) set to 1, which is not its value.

The two terms, against the ultraviolet scale

Statement: Theorem S1, built on Proposition S1 (the split), Lemma S4 (the vacuum term) and Lemma S5 (the horizon factor).

What this does not show. It does not show the true error — only the upper bound of Theorem S1, and for \(k\neq0\) only the shape of that bound, since \(C_0\) is not computed anywhere in the manuscript.

What is on the axes

Term (I), static — vacuum and field. Lemma S4 gives \((d_A+d_B)\,\eta_M(N)\) with

$$\eta_M(N)\le\begin{cases}\tfrac{\pi}{4}\,2^{-(N-M)} & \text{on the full algebra},\\[2pt] \tfrac{\pi^{2}}{16}\,2^{-2(N-M)} & \text{on the chiral algebra}.\end{cases}$$

It is a function of the depth \(r=N-M\) alone. On the full two-component algebra it is only first order, and it then dominates everything: this is why the chiral algebra is the interesting case.

Term (II), dynamics. \(\tfrac16 d_B\Theta_M|t|\varepsilon_N^2\) with \(\Theta_M=(\sum_{l\in\Gamma_M}\theta(\pm l)|l|^6)^{1/2}\). It is linear in the horizon — which holds only at \(k=0\), where \([L_0,H_0]=0\) makes the energy shell dynamically invariant — and it carries the observation scale through \(\Theta_M\).

The \(k\neq0\) branch. Proposition S2 assembles \(C\,\varepsilon_N^2\) with \(C\sim C_0(M,k,L,K)\,m^2\,\Lambda_k(T)\) and \(\Lambda_k(T)=(e^{c_kT}-1)/c_k\). Two things change at \(k\neq0\): the horizon factor stops being linear in \(T\), and \(C_0\) becomes an object that the supplement names but does not evaluate. The \(m^2\) comes from the telescoping — \(m\) terms, each already carrying one factor of \(m\) through the slot count of Lemma S3.

Inverting. \(C\,\varepsilon_N^2\le\delta\), i.e. \(C\,4^{-N}\le\delta\) once the fixed factor \(L^2\) is absorbed into \(C\), gives \(N\ge\tfrac12\log_2(C/\delta)\) and \(n\lesssim2C^{1/2}\delta^{-1/2}\). At \(k=0\) that inversion produces a number, and the readout prints it. At \(k\neq0\) it does not, because \(C_0\) is missing; the readout says so rather than inventing one.

\(c_k\) is a control on this page, not a prediction. The supplement gives only a one-particle bound, \(c_{\sigma,k}\le C_L\sigma|k|\langle k\rangle^{\sigma+2}\), and does not evaluate \(C_L\). What is certain is \(c_0=0\), and that \(c_{\pm1}\neq0\) because \([L_0,L_{\pm1}]=\mp L_{\pm1}\neq0\).
