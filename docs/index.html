Quantum Simulation of Conformal Field Theory

Quantum Simulation of Conformal Field Theory

Tobias J. Osborne and Alexander Stottmeister — Institut für Theoretische Physik, Leibniz Universität Hannover.

A quantum algorithm for the dynamics and the local conformal symmetries of a 1+1-dimensional conformal field theory, built on operator-algebraic renormalization, with an error budget and a resource count. This site is the documentation index for the Letter and its Supplemental Material, and it recomputes the quantitative claims in your browser so that they can be checked rather than taken on trust.

Read this first

Nothing here has been refereed. The manuscript is between submissions. The revision was produced by the authors working with an AI assistant, the working record is published rather than discarded, and human verification is ongoing. Direction and responsibility are the authors’.

The error analysis has two halves, and only one of them is established. For the chiral Hamiltonian evolution, \(k=0\), the correlator bound is proved with every constant in the revised companion manuscript. For the local conformal transformations, \(k\neq0\), the bound is assembled in the Supplemental Material out of cited inputs; its rate and its \(T\)- and \(m\)-dependence are what the assembly establishes, and the numerical value of its prefactor is not computed. That assembly has not been refereed and has not been independently audited.

The companion manuscript is itself unrefereed. Where this site says “established”, it means established in a cited manuscript — the published article Commun. Math. Phys. 398, 219 (2023) where the citation says so, and otherwise its revised and expanded version (v5), which is a preprint.

The distinction is carried through every page: every numbered statement is labelled, and the error-budget widget draws the established part and the assembled part differently.

The four numbers

Everything on this site hangs off these. Each is verified against the statement in the Supplemental Material that it comes from; the locators are in the table of anchors.

\(\delta^{-3/2}\)

The gate count, \(T^{1+o(1)}\delta^{-3/2-o(1)}\): a Heisenberg-limited measurement cost \(\delta^{-1}\) times a discretization cost \(\delta^{-1/2}\). Widget F

\(n = 2^{N+1}\), \(2n\) qubits

Sites at ultraviolet scale \(N\), two qubits per site under the Jordan–Wigner map. Widget B

\(\varepsilon_N^{2}\propto4^{-N}\)

The one-particle rate every leaf of the error budget carries along the momentum-cutoff route, and the rate the correlators are measured to obey. The constant depends on the circumference: see the two spacings. Widget H

128 sites, 256 qubits

The near-term point: a D10 wavelet at accuracy \(\delta=\tfrac12\) on a lattice of 128 sites, i.e. \(N=6\), certified at observation scale \(M=3\). Widget G

The two repositories

This work has a companion, Conformal field theory from lattice fermions, and the two sites divide the subject between them.

  • This site owns the algorithm and its cost: the four-step algorithm, the error budget of Theorem S1, the resource accounting and the near-term figure.
  • The companion site owns the mathematics of the approximation: the regularity thresholds, the sharp one-particle rates, the two renormalization routes, the energy bounds and the multiplicity criterion. The one-particle inputs this site's error budget consumes — Lemmas S1, S2, S4, S5 — are all proved there.

Rather than rebuild them, this site links across: its interactive companion, and the sources at github.com/alexander-stottmeister/lattice_cft. It is the target of the citation [v5] throughout this site, and its manuscript is served at free_fermion_cft_v5.pdf, so a statement cited as [v5, Proposition 6.3] can be opened and read.

Reproducing what is on this site

Every number the widgets check themselves against is in docs/data/, and every one of those is produced by one script:

python3 tools/make_data.py          # writes docs/data/*.json
python3 tools/make_data.py --check  # recompute and diff against what is committed
python3 tools/make_figures.py       # writes the static SVG fallbacks in docs/figures/
node    tools/check_js.mjs          # the browser's own kernels, run outside a browser
node    tools/check_pages.mjs       # load every page under DOM stubs, sweep every control

numpy and the standard library, nothing else; matplotlib is deliberately not used, and the static figures are SVG written directly. To read the site, serve the folder:

cd docs && python3 -m http.server 8000

A note on how the site was built, what was left out of it and what a reviewer should look at first is docs/README-SITE.md.

The site loads nothing from a third-party host. KaTeX is vendored under docs/assets/katex/, twenty woff2 faces included, so the site renders offline from a plain clone.
