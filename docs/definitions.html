Definitions

The objects the error analysis is about, in the order the Letter introduces them. Each entry says where it is defined and which widget makes it visible.

The simulated model

The staggered lattice and its Hamiltonian

A relativistic Dirac fermion on a circle of circumference \(2L\); periodic or antiperiodic boundary conditions realize the Ramond or Neveu–Schwarz sector, and the real chiral components of the massless theory give central charge \(c=\tfrac12\). The staggered-lattice approximation is \(\Lambda_N=\varepsilon_N\{-L_N,\dots,L_N-1\}\) with \(\varepsilon_NL_N=L\), carrying

$$H^{(N)}_{0}=\tfrac{L_N}{\pi}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_N}\Bigl(\psi^{(1)\dagger}_{x+\varepsilon_N}\psi^{(2)}_{x} -\psi^{(1)\dagger}_{x}\psi^{(2)}_{x}+\mathrm{h.c.} +\lambda_N\bigl(\psi^{(1)\dagger}_{x}\psi^{(1)}_{x}-\psi^{(2)\dagger}_{x}\psi^{(2)}_{x}\bigr)\Bigr).$$

The number of sites at scale \(N\) is \(n=2^{N+1}\), and the Hilbert space is a fermionic Fock space of dimension \(2^{2n}\).

Letter, Representing conformal fields on a quantum computer. — Widget B

The Jordan–Wigner map and the spin chain

Setting \(a_x\equiv\psi^{(1)}_x\) and \(a_{x+\varepsilon_{N+1}}\equiv\psi^{(2)\dagger}_x\) puts the fermions on the halved lattice \(\Lambda_{N+1}\), and the Jordan–Wigner string \(a_j\equiv\sigma^x_{-L}\cdots\sigma^x_{j-\varepsilon_{N+1}}\tfrac12(\sigma^z_j+i\sigma^y_j)\) turns the Hamiltonian into an XY chain in a transverse field:

$$H^{(N)}_{0}=\tfrac{L_N}{2\pi}\sum_{j\in\Lambda_{N+1}} \bigl(\sigma^z_j\sigma^z_{j+\varepsilon_{N+1}}-\sigma^y_j\sigma^y_{j+\varepsilon_{N+1}} +\lambda_N\sigma^x_j\bigr)+B_N,$$

where \(B_N\) carries the toroidal boundary conditions. This is where the factor of two in the qubit count comes from: \(n\) sites, two components each, \(2n\) qubits.

Letter, same paragraph. The Letter notes that more sophisticated encodings would improve the requirements. — Widget B

Operator-algebraic renormalization

The family \(\{\mathfrak A_N,\mathcal H_N,H^{(N)}_0\}_N\)
A quantum lattice system at every resolution \(\varepsilon=2^{-N}\): an algebra of observables \(\mathfrak A_N\) on a Hilbert space \(\mathcal H_N\) with a Hamiltonian.
Coarse-graining \(\mathcal E^{N_2}_{N_1}\) and the scaling map \(\alpha^{N_1}_{N_2}\)
The Schrödinger-picture coarse-graining between states at different scales, and its dual on observables. Unital and completely positive, so probability is preserved and partition functions of initial and renormalized states agree.
\(\rho^{(N)}_r=\mathcal E^{N+r}_N(\rho^{(N+r)}_0)\), the \(r\)th renormalization
The state on \(\mathfrak A_N\) obtained by coarse-graining an initial state from the finer scale \(N+r\). The subscript \(r\) counts renormalization steps — it is not a scale — while the superscript \(N\) is the fixed scale at which one observes.
The exact correlator as a scaling limit
The renormalized observables \(\widetilde A,\widetilde B\) live on \(\mathfrak A_M\) and are embedded into the simulated scale-\(N\) lattice by \(\alpha^M_N\); the exact correlator is the scaling limit at fixed observation scale \(M\) as \(N\to\infty\), equivalently as the number \(r=N-M\) of renormalization steps \(\to\infty\). The accuracy \(\delta\) bounds the deviation of the finite-\(N\) approximant from that limit.

Koo–Saleur generators

The Virasoro algebra is spanned by the \(L_k\) with \([L_k,L_l]=\tfrac L\pi(k-l)L_{k+l}+\tfrac c{12}\bigl((\tfrac L\pi k)^3-\tfrac L\pi k\bigr)\delta_{k+l,0}\); the CFT Hamiltonian is \(H^{\mathrm{CFT}}=\tfrac\pi L(L_0+\overline L_0-\tfrac c{12})\) and the momentum \(P^{\mathrm{CFT}}=\tfrac\pi L(L_0-\overline L_0)\).

Writing \(H^{(N)}_k=\varepsilon_N\tfrac L\pi\sum_x e^{ikx}h^{(N)}_x\) for the Fourier modes of the lattice Hamiltonian density, the Koo–Saleur ansatz is

$$L^{(N)}_k=\tfrac12\Bigl(H^{(N)}_k+\tfrac{\pi\frac12\varepsilon_N}{L\sin(\frac12\varepsilon_Nk)} \bigl[H^{(N)}_k,H^{(N)}_0\bigr]\Bigr),$$

with an analogous formula for \(\overline L^{(N)}_k\). Depending on the representation this realizes \(c=0\), \(\tfrac12\) or \(1\). The crucial feature for simulation is that the \(L^{(N)}_k\) are sums of densities, \((2k+1)\)-local in the momentum representation, so standard local-Hamiltonian simulation applies.

The one-particle symbol

In the plane-wave basis the matrix elements are explicit, and the whole discretization rate comes from one expansion:

$$\varepsilon_N^{-1}\sin\bigl(\varepsilon_N(l\mp\tfrac k2)\bigr)-\bigl(l\mp\tfrac k2\bigr) \;=\;O\bigl(\varepsilon_N^{2}\,|l\mp\tfrac k2|^{3}\bigr).$$

What is actually simulated

In a unitary positive-energy representation \(L_k^*=L_{-k}\), so \(L_k\) is not self-adjoint for \(k\neq0\) and generates no unitary group. The simulated dynamics is generated by the self-adjoint Hermitian combination

$$H_k:=L_k+L_{-k},\qquad H^{(N)}_k:=L^{(N)}_k+L^{(N)}_{-k},$$

essentially self-adjoint on every core for \(\mathbf 1+L_0\) by Nelson’s commutator theorem, so that \(U_t=e^{itH_k}\) and \(U^{(N)}_t=e^{itH^{(N)}_k}\) are genuine unitary groups. The Heisenberg evolutes are \(B_t=U_tBU_t^*\) and \(\widetilde B_t=U^{(N)}_t \widetilde BU^{(N)*}_t\).

supp., §Setup and §Well-posedness of the flows; Letter, Simulating dynamics and its footnote.

The two correlators being compared

$$C^{(N)}_t=\langle\Omega_N|\,\widetilde A\,\widetilde B_t\,|\Omega_N\rangle, \qquad C_t=\langle\Omega|\,A\,B_t\,|\Omega\rangle,$$

with the analogous \(m\)-point objects. The accuracy statement of the Letter is \(|C^{(N)}_t-C_t|\le\delta\). The intermediate object of the master decomposition,

$$\widetilde C^{(N)}_t=\langle\Omega|\,A\,\bigl(\widetilde U^{(N)}_tB\widetilde U^{(N)*}_t\bigr)\,|\Omega\rangle,$$

uses the continuum vacuum and continuum fields but the lattice-pushed approximating dynamics, and splitting at it separates the static error from the dynamical one.

supp., Prop. S1. — Widget E

The four atomic errors

  • field — \(\widetilde A-A\), \(\widetilde B-B\)
  • vacuum — \(\Omega_N\to\Omega\)
  • generator — \(\widetilde H^{(N)}_k-H_k\)
  • propagation — of the generator error through the flow \(U_t\) over \([0,T]\)

The ground-state circuit, in detail

The lattice vacuum is Gaussian, so

$$|\Omega_N\rangle=U_{\mathrm{FT}}\,U_{\mathrm B}\!\!\bigotimes_{k\in\Gamma_{N+1}}\!\!|{\leftarrow}\rangle_k .$$
  • \(U_{\mathrm B}\): after the fermionic Fourier transform the modes decouple into Nambu pairs \((k,-k)\), so \(U_{\mathrm B}\) is a product of \(O(n)\) two-mode Bogoliubov (Givens) rotations on disjoint qubit pairs — hence \(O(n)\) gates in depth \(O(1)\), no ancillas. The angles \(\theta_k\) come from a classical \(2\times2\) eigenproblem, \(O(n)\) arithmetic, once and for all.
  • \(U_{\mathrm{FT}}\): the fermionic fast Fourier transform, radix-2 butterflies built from matchgates: \(O(n\log n)\) two-qubit gates in depth \(O(\log^2 n)\). Without translation invariance the fallback is a Givens network at \(O(n^2)\) gates and depth \(O(n)\).
  • Exactness: the preparation is exact at the level of the Gaussian state; the only approximation is gate synthesis, \(O(\log(1/\delta_{\mathrm{syn}}))\) per gate, which is not the dominant cost.
