How to read the labels

established
Proved in a cited manuscript, with a locator. Most locators are [v5, …], the revised and expanded version of the companion paper, which is a preprint, not a refereed article. Where a locator points at Commun. Math. Phys. 398, 219 (2023), that statement is refereed — but see the corrections: three statements of the published article do not hold as printed and are cited only through the revision.
assembled here
Put together in the Supplemental Material from the cited inputs. The steps are written out; no independent audit has been carried out; nothing has been refereed. For the main case, \(k\neq0\), the numerical value of the prefactor is not computed — what the assembly establishes is the rate and the \(T\)- and \(m\)-dependence.
cited
Quoted from the literature unchanged and used as a black box.
numerical
Supported by a computation, not by a proof. The scripts are in the repository and this site recomputes them in your browser.

The numbered statements

Proposition S1 — master decomposition cited construction \(k\neq0\) bookkeeping assembled here

Insert between the simulated correlator and the continuum one the intermediate object that uses the continuum vacuum and continuum fields but the lattice-pushed approximating dynamics; the error then splits into a static term (I), vacuum and field, and a dynamical term (II), the generator difference and its propagation through the unbounded flow over \([0,T]\).

Supplemental Material, § Discretization error analysis, Prop. S1 and Eqs. (S4)–(S5). The decomposition itself is the one used in [CMP 398, Thm 6.1] and [v5, Prop. 6.3]; what the supplement adds is the bookkeeping of the constants for \(k\neq0\). — Widget E

Theorem S1 — quantitative discretization bound (target)

(i) Chiral Hamiltonian evolution, \(k=0\). established — [v5, Prop. 6.3]

For \(A,B\) monomials of degrees \(d_A,d_B\) in the lattice fermions at scale \(M\) and the dynamics generated by \(L_0\),

$$\bigl|C^{(N)}_{t}-C_{t}\bigr|\;\le\;(d_{A}+d_{B})\,\eta_{M}(N)\;+\;\tfrac16\,d_{B}\,\Theta_{M}\,|t|\,\varepsilon_{N}^{2},$$

with \(\eta_{M}(N)\le\tfrac{\pi}{4}2^{-(N-M)}\) on the full algebra and \(\eta_{M}(N)\le\tfrac{\pi^{2}}{16}2^{-2(N-M)}\) on the chiral algebra, and \(\Theta_{M}=\bigl(\sum_{l\in\Gamma_{M}}\theta(\pm l)|l|^{6}\bigr)^{1/2}\) explicit. Uniform on \([0,T]\) with a prefactor linear in \(T\); for chiral observables both terms are of order \(4^{-(N-M)}\). Every constant is there.

(ii) Local conformal transformations, \(k\neq0\). assembled here, unaudited

There are \(C=C(T,M,k,m,L,K)<\infty\) and \(N_0\) such that for all \(N\ge N_0\) and all \(0\le t_1,\dots,t_m\le T\),

$$\bigl|C^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{m}}-C_{t_{1},\dots,t_{m}}\bigr|\;\le\;C\,\varepsilon_{N}^{2}, \qquad C\sim C_{0}(M,k,L,K)\,m^{2}\,\Lambda_{k}(T),\qquad \Lambda_{k}(T)=\frac{e^{c_{k}T}-1}{c_{k}}.$$

\(c_0=0\), so \(\Lambda_0(T)=T\). \(C_0\) is not computed. What the assembly establishes is the rate \(\varepsilon_N^2\) and the \(T\)- and \(m\)-dependence.

Consequently, to reach accuracy \(\delta\) it suffices that \(C\,4^{-N}\le\delta\), i.e. \(N(\delta,T)\ge\tfrac12\log_2(C/\delta)\) and \(n(\delta,T)=2^{N+1}\lesssim 2C^{1/2}\delta^{-1/2}\).

Supplemental Material, Thm. S1 and Eqs. (S6)–(S8). — Widget E, Widget F

Lemma S1 — one-particle generator rate established — [v5, Lem. 4.15, Thm. 4.16]

Along the momentum-cutoff renormalization group, \(\lVert(\widetilde\ell^{(N)}_{\pm,k}-\ell_{\pm,k})\xi\rVert\le C_1(k,L)\,\varepsilon_N^{\min\{\delta_s,2\}}\lVert\xi\rVert_{h^{1+\delta_s}}\), and the rate is sharp. Two-regular smearing (\(K\ge5\)) gives \(\delta_s=2\), hence \(\varepsilon_N^2\). The mechanism is the whole story of the rate: the symbol difference is \(\varepsilon_N^{-1}\sin(\varepsilon_N(l\mp\tfrac k2))-(l\mp\tfrac k2) =O(\varepsilon_N^{2}|l\mp\tfrac k2|^{3})\).

Along the wavelet route the rate is \(\varepsilon_N^2\) for \(k=0\) but only \(\varepsilon_N\) for \(k\neq0\) [v5, Rmk. 4.8, Rmk. 4.32], unless the comparison is re-centred [v5, Rmk. 3.5]. The published domination argument [CMP 398, Lem. 4.5, eqs. (204)–(210)] does not hold as stated and is replaced in [v5, Lem. 3.14, Cor. 3.15]. — Widget C

Lemma S2 — off-diagonal Hilbert–Schmidt part established — [v5, Lem. 4.15, Rmk. 4.22]

\(\lVert(\widetilde\ell^{(N)}_{\pm,k}-\ell_{\pm,k})_{\pm\mp}\rVert_2^2\le C_2(k)\varepsilon_N^4\), a sum over the finite strip \(0<|l|<|k|\), which has \(2(|k|-1)\) points and is empty for \(k=0,\pm1\): in the Neveu–Schwarz sector the Möbius generators \(L_{-1},L_0,L_1\) incur no off-diagonal cost.

The stronger published claim [CMP 398, Rmk. 4.14], that for the Möbius generators the wavelet route alone suffices, does not follow from its proof; only the vanishing of the Hilbert–Schmidt part is used. Figure S5 of the supplement is this quantity. — Widget J

Lemma S3 — second-quantization lift cited — [CMP 398, eq. (200)]

Because \(L^{(N)}_k\) is bilinear in the fermions, its action on a \(p\)-particle vector is a sum over the \(p\) slots plus the two pair-creation/annihilation slots: the diagonal blocks enter through their one-particle norm and the off-diagonal blocks through their Hilbert–Schmidt norm, with an explicit slot prefactor \((p+2)\). By Lemmas S1–S2 the right-hand side is \(\le C_3(k,L)(p+2)\varepsilon_N^2\prod_q\lVert\eta_q\rVert_M\).

Unaffected by the revision. This per-generator factor of the particle number is what later produces the \(O(m^2)\) growth of the assembled constant.

Lemma S4 — lattice-vacuum rate established — [v5, Prop. 6.3, Cor. 3.19]

For \(O\) a monomial of degree \(d\) at scale \(M\), \(|\omega^{(N)}_{0,\pm}(\alpha^M_N(O))-\omega_\pm(\alpha^M_\infty(O))|\le d\,\eta_M(N)\), with \(\eta_M(N)=\max_{l\in\Gamma_M}\lVert S^{(N)}_0(l)-S(l)\rVert\le\tfrac\pi4 2^{-(N-M)}\) on the full two-component algebra and \(\le\tfrac{\pi^2}{16}2^{-2(N-M)}\) on the chiral algebra.

The consequence the whole site turns on: both error sources scale in the renormalization depth \(r=N-M\), not in \(N\) alone [v5, Rmk. 6.4(1)]. — Widget A

Lemma S5 — uniform linear energy bounds and energy growth established — [v5, Lem. 4.17, Prop. 6.6, Lem. 4.19]

For \(1\)-regular \(s\) (\(K\ge3\)) there are constants \(b_k,c_k<\infty\) independent of the scale \(N\) with \(\lVert L_k^{(N)}\Psi\rVert\le b_k\lVert\mathcal{N}^{(N)}\Psi\rVert\) and \(\pm H_k^{(N)}\le b_k\mathcal{N}^{(N)}\) as quadratic forms; consequently the flow propagates the energy at most exponentially, \(\lVert(\mathcal{N}^{(N)})^{p}e^{itH_k^{(N)}}\Psi\rVert\le e^{p c_k|t|}\lVert(\mathcal{N}^{(N)})^{p}\Psi\rVert\), with \(c_0=0\) because \([L_0,H_0]=0\).

These are the lattice counterparts, uniform in the scale, of the Virasoro linear energy bounds of Buchholz–Schulz-Mirbach and Carpi. They close the gap in the published proof of [CMP 398, Thm. 6.3], where such a bound had been asserted. A state of bounded energy therefore stays, for all \(t\le T\), inside a horizon-dependent but \(N\)-independent energy shell, on which the static rates apply uniformly. — Widget I

Proposition S2 — assembly of the target rate assembled here, unaudited

Under the hypotheses of Theorem S1(ii) — a free-fermion CFT, the momentum-cutoff route with \(2\)-regular smearing, a generator order \(k\), a localization scale \(M\), an \(m\)-point configuration and a horizon \(T\) — the static estimates (Lemmas S1–S4), the dynamics module (Lemma S5 and the quantitative Duhamel estimate) and the \(m\)-point telescoping combine to \(|C^{(N)}_{\mathbf t}-C_{\mathbf t}|\le C\varepsilon_N^2\) with \(C\) left in factored form: the one-particle constant \(C_1(k,L)\), the horizon factor, the combinatorial \(m^2\) and the Sobolev weight of the field vectors.

This is the statement to be sceptical of. The supplement says so itself: “What is established is the rate \(\varepsilon_N^2\) together with the explicit \(T\)- and \(m\)-dependence; the numerical value of the prefactor for \(k\neq0\) is not computed here.” For \(k=0\) and two-point functions of monomials the statement reduces to Theorem S1(i), where nothing is assembled. — Widget E
