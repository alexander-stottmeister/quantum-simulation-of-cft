Notation

The symbols, as the Supplemental Material fixes them, plus the two places where this site has to choose a convention that the manuscript leaves implicit. Those two are flagged, because a widget that computes a number under an unstated convention is worse than no widget.

The three scales

\(N\) — the ultraviolet lattice cutoff scale
The negative logarithm of the lattice spacing. The lattice at scale \(N\) has \(n=2^{N+1}\) sites and is simulated on \(2n=2^{N+2}\) qubits.
supp., §Discretization error analysis, “Throughout”
\(M\) — the observation, or localization, scale
The spatial or momentum localization of the observables whose correlator is wanted. Always \(M<N\). The Letter’s Complexity paragraph takes \(M=N-1\), which fixes the maximal observation scale the register can host, not the accuracy.
supp., §The near-term figure, closing parenthesis; Letter, Complexity
\(r=N-M\) — the renormalization depth
The number of renormalization steps between the observation scale and the simulated scale. Both error sources of the master decomposition scale in \(r\), not in \(N\) alone.
supp., Lem. S4, Source and mechanism [v5, Rmk. 6.4(1)]

Wavelets and regularity

\(K\), written D\(K\) in the figures
The number of vanishing moments of the Daubechies scaling function \(s={}_Ks\).
\(\sigma_K=\sup\{\sigma:\ s\in H^{\sigma}(\mathbb R)\}\)
Its Sobolev exponent; \(s\) is \(\rho\)-regular if \(\sigma_K>\rho\). Numerically \(\sigma_K = 1.000,\ 1.415,\ 1.776,\ 2.098,\ 2.390,\ 2.661,\ 2.917,\ 3.165,\ 3.406\) for \(K=2,\dots,10\), so 2-regularity needs \(K\ge5\) and 3-regularity \(K\ge9\).
supp., §Discretization error analysis, “Throughout” [v5, Def. 3.12, Rmk. 3.13]
\(\delta_{\!s}\)
The regularity of the smeared observables, which is what the one-particle rate \(\varepsilon_N^{\min\{\delta_s,2\}}\) saturates. Two-regular smearing gives \(\delta_s=2\).

A collision to watch. In the published companion article the regularity exponent is itself written \(\delta\). The supplement avoids this, because there \(\delta\) is the accuracy; and the legend of Fig. S3 still labels a Sobolev exponent \(\delta\), which the caption relabels \(s\). On this site \(\delta\) is always the accuracy.

Two conventions this site has to fix

The mode set \(\Gamma_N\)

The supplement writes \(\Gamma_M\) in \(\Theta_M\) and in \(\eta_M(N)\) without spelling out the normalization. This site uses the convention of the manuscript’s own numerics script, numerics_correlator.py:

$$\Gamma_N=\{\,l\in\mathbb Z+\tfrac12\ :\ |l|<2^{N}\,\},\qquad |\Gamma_N| = 2^{N+1}=n,\qquad \varepsilon_N=2^{-N},$$

i.e. half-integer (Neveu–Schwarz) modes, one per lattice site. This makes \(\varepsilon_N^2=4^{-N}\), which is what the supplement writes. The absolute value of \(\Theta_M\) depends on this choice — it is a sum over \(\Gamma_M\) of \(|l|^6\) — so the number that widget E prints for \(\Theta_M\) is convention-dependent even though the \(4^{-N}\) rate is not. The widget says so.

A different convention is in use in the companion repository, where \(\varepsilon=\pi 2^{-N}\) and \(|l|<\pi/\varepsilon\). The rates agree; the constants do not. Do not carry a constant across without converting.

The two renormalization routes

“Momentum-cutoff” and “wavelet” are two renormalization groups, not two approximations of one. They give different rates for \(k\neq0\) (\(\varepsilon_N^2\) versus \(\varepsilon_N\), unless the comparison is re-centred) and hence different resource exponents (\(\delta^{-1/2}\) versus \(\delta^{-1}\) sites). Every widget states which route it is on; the near-term figure is on the wavelet route at \(k=0\), while the resource scaling is on the momentum-cutoff route.
